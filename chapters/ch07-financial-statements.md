# Chapter 7: Financial Statements

## Core Idea

Augment standardized financial statements with visual decomposition while preserving accounting accuracy, line-item traceability, and conventional totals. Use connected flows to explain how components produce balances across periods.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Summary, Detail, and Multi-year Waterfalls**
  - When to use: Explaining how positive and negative flows produce a subtotal or ending balance.
  - How: Use a common baseline and explicit subtotals; show only major categories in summary, underlying line items in detail, and aligned years in multi-year form.
- **Cascade Variants**
  - When to use: Emphasizing direction and impact with a flow-like form.
  - How: Preserve the arithmetic and subtotal structure of the waterfall while using consistent directional shapes.
- **Integrated Statements**
  - When to use: Linking cash flow, operating budget, or activity statements.
  - How: Reuse matching categories, scales, and selected periods; make cross-statement connections visible without merging incompatible accounting definitions.
- **Transparency + Accessibility**
  - When to use: Any visual financial statement.
  - How: Reveal source line items and calculations; provide direct access to detail; keep the standardized table available for exact values.

## Key Concepts

- **Subtotal**: Intermediate aggregate whose components must reconcile.
- **Ending balance**: Result after all inflows and outflows.
- **Summary Waterfall**: Major components and subtotals only.
- **Detail Waterfall**: Individual line items plus subtotals.
- **Integrated Waterfall**: Coordinated flow linking related statements or sections.
- **Offset Area Bar**: Encodes change from a starting point using filled area.

## Mental Models

- A financial-statement visual is an explanation layered on an accounting record.
- Every visible total needs a route to the contributing lines.
- Straight baselines and aligned years communicate precision.
- Reuse one flow grammar across statements when the arithmetic relationship is the same.

## Anti-patterns

- Hiding line items to create a cleaner story without declaring aggregation.
- Using disconnected bars that make cumulative arithmetic difficult to follow.
- Comparing years with different category definitions or scales.
- Replacing the authoritative statement with a visual that omits exact values.
- Linking statements that are conceptually related but do not reconcile numerically.

## Worked Example

To explain annual cash movement, start at beginning cash, add operating and investing inflows, subtract outflows, and end at year-end cash. A summary view displays the main categories; selection opens the detailed line items. A multi-year view aligns annual ending balances and preserves the same color/sign rules. If an operating budget contributes to a major cash-flow subtotal, link the matching category and allow the user to inspect the budget composition without leaving the cash-flow context.

## Reference Table

| Variant | Show | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| Summary Waterfall | Major components + subtotals | Executive explanation |
| Detail Waterfall | Line items + subtotals | Analyst or audit inspection |
| Multi-year Waterfall | Repeated aligned annual flows | Trend and structural change |
| Cascade | Directional component flow | Shape reinforces inflow/outflow |
| Integrated Waterfall | Linked statement sections | Users need cross-statement causes |

## Key Takeaways

1. Preserve reconciliation and traceability.
2. Separate summary communication from detail inspection.
3. Keep sign, subtotal, and year alignment stable.
4. Use visuals to augment, not replace, authoritative statements.
