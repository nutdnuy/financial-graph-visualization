# Chapter 8: Pension Funds

## Core Idea

Use coordinated historical, demographic, cash-flow, and funding views to assess a pension plan's future obligations. Curate views to emphasize the specific planning question rather than reproducing actuarial tables wholesale.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Full Table -> Curated Perspective**
  - When to use: Transforming actuarial or annual-report tables.
  - How: Begin with the complete data inventory; identify the planning question; select the variables and comparison that directly answer it; retain access to the source table.
- **Absolute + Relative Pairing**
  - When to use: A level and a ratio tell different parts of the pension story.
  - How: Align member counts, benefits, contributions, assets, or liabilities with growth rates, ratios, or benchmark bands.
- **Flow Variants for Additions and Deductions**
  - When to use: Explaining net-position changes over time.
  - How: Choose Fold-Over, Bar Shadow, Bar Connectors, Directional Waterfall, or Folded Bar according to whether the question is composition, continuity, or direction.
- **Future-State Planning View**
  - When to use: Connecting current demographics and funding to future requirements.
  - How: Show member composition, trend, contributions/benefits, and funding ratio with consistent periods and scenarios.

## Key Concepts

- **Members in valuation**: Population used in actuarial assessment.
- **Contributions versus benefits**: Inflows and outflows affecting plan sustainability.
- **Funding ratio**: Assets relative to liabilities or obligations under a defined method.
- **Area Comparison**: Separate aligned area charts used to compare levels or populations.
- **Net Change Bar Shadow**: Foreground net change with background component quantities.
- **Directional Waterfall**: Cumulative additions and deductions with explicit direction.

## Mental Models

- A pension view is a future-capacity argument built from present levels and historical trends.
- Full tables preserve evidence; curated views reveal a question.
- Pair payment flows with member composition and funding context.
- Use familiar bars and areas before introducing complex custom forms.

## Anti-patterns

- Showing a funding ratio without its calculation basis or history.
- Comparing membership and payment series with inconsistent periods.
- Presenting cumulative and annual flows on one ambiguous scale.
- Omitting the source table or actuarial definition.
- Treating a visually dramatic trend as a forecast without assumptions.

## Worked Example

A plan sponsor asks whether retiree growth is outpacing active membership and whether contributions cover benefits. Use aligned area/line views for member groups, then a directional flow view for annual contributions, benefits, and net change. Add the funding ratio as a separate contextual view, not as proof of causation. Keep the actuarial basis and period visible. If users select a year, coordinate highlights across demographic, cash-flow, and funding views.

## Reference Table

| Question | View family |
|---|---|
| How is the member population changing? | Aligned lines/areas and demographic segmentation |
| What composes annual net change? | Bar Shadow or Directional Waterfall |
| How does each year connect to the next? | Bar Connectors or Folded Bar |
| Is the plan's funding position changing? | Ratio plus assets/liabilities context |
| Which detail explains an aggregate? | Zoom/drill-down with source table |

## Key Takeaways

1. Coordinate demographics, flows, and funding without conflating them.
2. Use historical context and explicit actuarial definitions.
3. Curate each view for a planning question.
4. Preserve the evidence behind aggregate ratios.
